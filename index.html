Research

Localization as allocation for contaminated retrieval

Undergraduate thesis and independent research · 2026

Contamination in retrieval-augmented generation is sparse: an adversary places payloads in a few segments, not the corpus. Writing expected attack success as E[ASR] = p₀ − h·(p₀ − p₁) shows the benefit of a test-time defense turns on the hit rate of its allocator, so it needs only to out-rank random allocation rather than to classify text correctly. A training-free localizer built on a lexical and a semantic signal reduced mean attack success from 88.9% to 2.65% at 0.67× the undefended token cost while retaining about 88% of clean-task utility, across five open-weight models and two multi-hop QA benchmarks.
